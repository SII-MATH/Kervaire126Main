## Proof of the Mixed-Product Bound

### 1. The whole inner bracket has filtration at least 10

Let

\[
                       B=\langle\alpha,2,\theta_4\rangle\subset\pi_{93}.
\]

It is defined since `2 alpha=2 theta_4=0`. On E4 its three inputs have
degrees `(6,68)`, `(1,1)`, `(2,32)`. Their products vanish on E3: the first
is zero already in Ext because `h_0 n=0`, and the second is killed by
`d_2(h_5)=h_0 h_4^2`. Zero preceding-page defining systems are allowed.
The E4 Massey output degree is

\[
                 (6,68)+(1,1)+(2,32)-(3,2)=(6,99).
\]

The complete Moss crossing checks are:

* For `alpha*2`, the product filtration is 7 and stem is 62. On E4 a
  crossing would need a source in stem 63 with `q<7-(4-1)=4` and a later
  differential with target filtration strictly greater than 7. The only
  such low-filtration classes are the three `d_2` sources listed in input 3
  and the permanent class `h_1 h_5^2`. There is no crossing.
* For `2*theta_4`, the product filtration is 3. The E4 bound is `q<0`, so
  no source is possible.

Moss gives some `gamma_1 in B` in `F^6 pi_93=F^10 pi_93`. This is an
existence statement for one bracket member; we next control the full
indeterminacy to obtain the assertion for all members.

The indeterminacy of B is

\[
                   \alpha\pi_{31}+\pi_{63}\theta_4.
\]

In the first summand, the `eta theta_4` generator gives zero because
`alpha theta_4=0`; the `n` and `rho_31` contributions lie in F11 and F17.
For the second summand, every element of `pi_63` is, modulo F4, a multiple
of `eta theta`. The Ext equation `h_1 h_5^2 h_4^2=0` puts
`eta theta theta_4` in F6. Multiplying the F4 remainder by theta4 also puts
it in F6. The stem-93 gap raises both contributions to F10. Therefore

\[
                               B\subset F^{10}\pi_{93}.
\]

For every `gamma in B`, full shuffle also gives

\[
 \gamma\theta_4\in
 \langle\alpha,2,\theta_4\rangle\theta_4
 =\alpha\langle2,\theta_4,\theta_4\rangle=\{0\}.
\]

The last bracket is defined using `theta_4^2=0` and is zero using `pi_61=0`.
Thus all of the outer brackets used next are genuinely defined.

### 2. Each outer bracket lies in F13

For `gamma in B`, consider

\[
                   T_\gamma=\langle\gamma,\theta_4,2\rangle\subset\pi_{124}.
\]

Regard gamma as a class of filtration at least 10, using zero detection if
its leading grade-10 class is zero. The corresponding E3 products vanish:
input 4 gives the product with `h_4^2` already zero in Ext, and
`h_4^2 h_0` is the stated `d_2` boundary. The Massey degree is

\[
                (10,103)+(2,32)+(1,1)-(2,1)=(11,135).
\]

For `gamma*theta_4`, the product filtration is 12, stem 123. Moss on E3
only examines sources in stem 124 with `q<10`, and requires the target
filtration to be strictly greater than 12. The complete finite source
window is:

| Source filtration q | Recorded possibilities | Why no crossing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0--5 | Empty | No source |
| 6 | One nonzero d5 | Target filtration 11, below 12 |
| 7 | d3, d5, or an earlier boundary | The d5 target has filtration exactly 12 |
| 8 | Nonzero d2 | Already absent on E3 |
| 9 | d2 or d3 | Absent on E4, no later crossing |

The equality in the q=7 row is essential: the strict Moss bound does not
count this differential as a crossing. The other product `theta_4*2` has
filtration 3, and its E3 crossing range `q<1` in stem 31 is empty.

Moss consequently supplies a member of `T_gamma` in F11. Its whole
indeterminacy is

\[
                        \gamma\pi_{31}+2\pi_{124}.
\]

The first summand lies in F13. The second lies in F11 because
`pi_124=F10 pi_124` and 2 has Adams filtration 1. Thus the entire bracket
lies in F11, which equals F13 by input 5:

\[
                              T_\gamma\subset F^{13}\pi_{124}.
\]

### 3. Insert the fourfold representative of theta5

Choose `theta in <2,theta_4,theta_4,2>`. Since `2 alpha=0`, the fourfold
product inclusion gives

\[
 \alpha\theta\in
 \alpha\langle2,\theta_4,\theta_4,2\rangle
 \subseteq\langle\langle\alpha,2,\theta_4\rangle,\theta_4,2\rangle.
\]

Every outer bracket on the right lies in F13 by the preceding steps.
Therefore `alpha theta in F13 pi_124`.

### 4. Remove both choices

First replace theta by any theta5 representative theta'. Their difference
lies in F6 pi62. Since alpha lies in F6, its product with this difference
lies in F12 pi124, which is F13. Hence `alpha theta' in F13`.

Next replace alpha by an arbitrary a6 detecting h5 n. Write
`a6=alpha+delta`, with `delta in F7 pi62=F8 pi62`. The grade-8 quotient is
one-dimensional, and input 6 says that its product with the grade-2 class
`h_5^2` is zero. Consequently `theta' delta in F11 pi124=F13 pi124`.
Thus `theta' a6 in F13`, proving the asserted universal mixed-product bound.

The same Ext calculation gives `theta5*a8 in F13` for the filtration-8
direction. The filtration-10 direction has product with theta5 in
F12=F13 directly. In particular, multiplication by theta5 sends the entire
higher-filtration subgroup F6 pi62 into F13 pi124.

## What This Does and Does Not Decide About AF10

125-shifted top copy. Conditional on the recorded survival of u to E10,
ordinary Leibniz shows inductively that these two copies have the ordinary
sphere differentials through d9. Thus the nonzero sphere class c is not
destroyed by an earlier cross-cell differential in this range. Unlike the
separate C2 d11 investigation, this candidate is not merely being assumed
nonzero because its row has level 9000. Its status follows from this module
comparison and the cited nonzero sphere E-infinity class. Formal delivery
of the actual cofiber and its comparison remains outside the current Lean
interfaces.

There is a natural map `C(theta5^2) -> C(theta5)` induced by the factorization
`theta5^2=theta5 theta5`. It is identity on the bottom cell and has top-cell
map `Sigma theta5`, so its top E2 image is zero. In `C(theta5)`, the finite
probe finds two still-uncomputed possible differential patterns involving
