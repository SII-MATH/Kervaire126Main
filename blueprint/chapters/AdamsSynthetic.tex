\chapter{AdamsSynthetic --- The Adams and Synthetic Framework}
We work with the classical stable category $\mathrm{Sp}$, the synthetic category $\mathrm{Syn}$, and their mod-two Adams towers. Fix a map $\nu_3\colon S^3\to S$ and a pointed detecting spectrum $D$. Let $\mathcal C_0$ consist of the sphere, the cofiber of $\nu_3$, $D$, and their integer suspensions. Let $\mathcal S_0$ be generated by the synthetic sphere and $\nu(\mathcal C_0)$ under bidegree shifts and finite $\lambda$ quotients. Convergence assertions below are restricted to these object families unless further hypotheses are stated.

In the statements quoted from \cite{LWX126}, spectra are assumed to be $2$-completed, connective, and of finite type. Original numbering and context are available in \href{paper/main.tex.html}{the source text} and \href{paper/2412.10879.pdf}{the paper}.

\section{The fixed classical and synthetic background}

\begin{definition}[The fixed categories, coefficients and cofibers]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:categories}
\level{coarse}
Let $\mathrm{Sp}$ and $\mathrm{Syn}$ be the classical and synthetic stable categories, with functorial cofibers. Equip $\mathrm{Sp}$ with its closed symmetric monoidal structure, and fix the mod-two coefficient object $H\mathbb F_2$ and its unit.

For $f\colon X\to Y$ in $\mathrm{Sp}$, choose a cofiber $C(f)$ with a distinguished triangle $X\xrightarrow f Y\to C(f)\to\Sigma X$. Every commutative square between such maps induces a map of cofibers commuting with the inclusions and boundaries.

Equip $\mathrm{Syn}$ with a preadditive, monoidal, pretriangulated category structure, a zero object, and additive integer suspension. For $p\in\mathbb Z^2$, the fully faithful functor $\Sigma^p$ has comparisons $\Sigma^q\Sigma^p\cong\Sigma^{p+q}$, $\Sigma^{0,0}\cong\operatorname{id}$, and $\Sigma^{n,0}\cong\Sigma^n$. There is a natural transformation $\lambda\colon\Sigma^{0,-1}\to\operatorname{id}$, and natural comparisons $\Sigma^p(X\otimes Y)\cong(\Sigma^pX)\otimes Y$.

For $f\colon X\to Y$ in $\mathrm{Syn}$, choose a cofiber $C(f)$ and a distinguished triangle $X\xrightarrow f Y\to C(f)\to\Sigma X$. A commutative square induces a map of these cofibers commuting with inclusion and boundary.

Choose $H\mathbb F_2\in\mathrm{Sp}$ together with an additive isomorphism $\pi_0H\mathbb F_2\cong\mathbb Z/2$ and $\pi_nH\mathbb F_2=0$ for every nonzero integer $n$. Its coefficient unit $S\to H\mathbb F_2$ is the class corresponding to $1\in\mathbb Z/2$.

The monoidal category $\mathrm{Sp}$ is symmetric and closed. For each $W$, tensoring on the right with $W$ and taking the internal hom $F(W,-)$ commute with integer suspension and preserve distinguished triangles for these suspension comparisons.

\begin{theorem}[Support and separation for finite spectra]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:finite-separation}
\level{coarse}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:filtration-comparison,sem:AdamsSynthetic:families,sem:AdamsSynthetic:selected-convergence}
Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a map between finite spectra. If $f$ lies in every integer stage of the Adams filtration, then $f$ is odd torsion. The same assertion holds for the factorization filtration. Consequently, every map between finite spectra which is not odd torsion has an exact finite integer filtration.

For $X\in\mathcal C_0$, one has $E_r^{s,t}(X)=0$ whenever $s<0$. For the sphere, $E_r^{0,t}(S)=0$ for every nonzero $t\geq0$. A sphere homotopy class lying in $F^s$ for every $s\in\mathbb N$ is odd torsion.

\lean{KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Standard.finite_map_separation,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Standard.separated_factorization,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Standard.finite_filtration_of_not_odd}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:filtration-comparison,sem:AdamsSynthetic:families}
\textit{Proof sketch and comparison scope.}
The finite-map statement is the separation input for the actual ordinary
Adams tower. The factorization version follows by the filtration comparison.
A map which is not odd torsion therefore cannot lie in every stage;
monotonicity and the lower bound on the filtration give its exact finite
stage. The support and sphere statements use the same coefficient tower.
Their source-to-fixed-model constructions remain the registered upstream
inputs; no stronger separation of untruncated synthetic objects is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Homology short exact sequences and their Ext boundaries]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:homology-ext-boundary}
\level{coarse}
Use the actual homology and derived Ext coordinates of

triangle $X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gZ\xrightarrow h\Sigma X$,
suppose its rotated homology sequence is short exact in each integer degree.
Then the short complex with the same maps $\mathcal H(g),\mathcal H(h)$
is short exact in the graded comodule category. Write its extension class as
\[
 [\mathcal S]\in

\begin{remark}[The target coset of a page extension]
\label{paper:5.7}
\label{def:fErextess}
\level{coarse}
From \cite{LWX126}, Remark 5.7.\par
Let $d_n^{f,E_r}(x)=y$ be an extension as in Definition~\ref{paper:5.4}, and put $e=e(f)$. The target $y$ is a coset with indeterminacy
\[
B_{1+n-e}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)
\]
together with the images of the shorter $d_{<n}^{\widehat f_{r-1}}$ extensions in that same extension spectral sequence. The extension is essential precisely when this full target coset does not contain zero. This is a statement about the coset, rather than a nonvanishing condition on a chosen representative in $E_2(Y)$.
For the level-$k$ relation of
Definition~\ref{sem:AdamsSynthetic:page-extension-levels}, the corresponding
boundary denominator is $B_{k+n-e+1}$ and the shorter images are taken
in that same quotient and weight. Essentiality again means that the full
target coset excludes zero. Changing $k$ changes this coset; a relation
transported by $\lambda$ or quotient reduction need not retain its
essentiality.
\uses{paper:5.4,paper:2.5,sem:AdamsSynthetic:page-extension-levels}
\end{remark}
\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{paper:5.4} reads the extension in the normalized-map ESS. Its target coordinates first divide out the displayed classical boundary group and then the images of shorter extensions. Essentiality means that the resulting differential target is nonzero, equivalently that the full coset does not contain zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Synthetic convergence]
